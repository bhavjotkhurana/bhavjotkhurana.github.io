About

After graduating from Reed College with a degree in Mathematics and Statistics, I made a deliberate choice: before building tools for learners, I wanted to understand them. 700+ tutoring sessions later, I have a clear picture of where and why people get stuck in math. Most students do not have a broad math problem; they have a gap in a specific foundational concept that was never caught, and once that gap is identified, progress is measurable and fast.

That instinct for what actually moves a student got rigorous at Yuno Learning, where the job was to prove whether an intervention worked rather than assume it did — A/B tests on content cadence and deadlines (t-tests, Cohen's d) and conversion tracking from a first website visit through to a tutor match. I learned to trust measurement over intuition.

Current focus: VLTG (vltg.net), a free diagnostic for a high-stakes trade licensing exam that I build and grow solo. Part of why I started it is the data: I've spent years working with education data and most of it is stale and second-hand, so I wanted to build something of my own and generate a live dataset I could actually work with. It puts the whole loop in my hands: the product, the analytics, and the experiments that tell me whether any of it is working.

Technical toolkit: Python, R, SQL, statistical modeling, experimentation design, A/B testing, and funnel/event instrumentation.

Outside of that, I spend my time powerlifting, following automotive design, and checking out cool places around Chicago for a chill work session. I started chicagoworkspots.com to collect work-friendly spots, initially for me and my partner. One simple but important feature is a power outlet rating based on the often-overlooked seat-to-outlet ratio. Feel free to check it out and add your own suggestions!
